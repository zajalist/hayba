# Scene Map + blockout + PLUMB: feasibility assessment

Date: 2026-09-22. Primary research papers and Epic's UE 5.8 documentation were checked directly. This is a product and engineering assessment, not a claim that the cited research already ships production game levels.

## Verdict

**Go** for one editable *Scene Intent Graph* that joins the Scene Map, blockout proposals, spatial/functional relationships, and PLUMB verdicts. **No-go** for a literal “5D point cloud” as the canonical data model, and **no-go** for marketing an AI latent as a reliable producer of finished scenes. The useful product is an interactive plan with explicit objects, spaces, relations, provenance, and measurable constraints. A colored point cloud is one optional view of that plan.

“Dimensions” here are different kinds of information, not interchangeable numerical axes. Position and extent live in 3D coordinates. “Chair faces reception,” “bed belongs to ward,” and “room connects to corridor” are typed graph edges. Time is an object/room state or event interval. Mood is an art-direction goal inferred from lighting, palette, prop density, composition, audio, and reference images; it cannot be read from one extra coordinate or certified by a geometry check. A learned embedding can rank assets and suggest relations, but its distances are not a designer-editable proof of functional or visual quality. This decomposition matches [Kimera's layered metric-semantic dynamic graph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.06894), [ConceptGraphs' object-centric 3D graph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16650), and [SceneFactor's editable semantic proxy boxes feeding a latent geometry generator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01801).

## Closest research and what it does *not* prove

| Work | Relevant result | Limit for Hayba |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [3DSSG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03967), [Kimera](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.06894), [Hydra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13360) | Point-cloud/metric geometry plus explicit objects, rooms, places, and relations is an established representation for understanding and planning. | These systems primarily infer an existing world from scans. They do not prove that a proposed game level is good. |
| [OpenScene](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15654), [OpenGaussian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02058) | Per-point or per-Gaussian semantic features support language queries and segmentation. | Searchable pixels/points do not give stable object identity, functional relationships, collisions, navigation, or editable Unreal assets. |
| [ConceptGraphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16650), [Open3DSG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12259) | Object nodes with geometry/embeddings and relation edges are close to the proposed “chair segment connected to other objects.” | ConceptGraphs reports mistaken captions, missing small/thin objects, duplicate detections, and LLM cost. In its paper, dense per-point features are explicitly described as memory-heavy and awkward to update. |
| [3D Gaussian Splatting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04079), [4D Gaussian Splatting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08528) | Efficient visual reconstruction; the “fourth dimension” in 4D-GS is *time-dependent appearance/geometry*. | Splats are visual evidence, not a game-ready scene graph, collision mesh, navmesh, or arbitrary semantics dimension. A splat can be a reference layer, never the sole blockout record. |
| [ATISS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03675), [DiffuScene](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14207), [LayoutGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15393), [Holodeck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09067) | Layout generation from room types, floor plans, text, object sets, and spatial constraints is real. Holodeck uses LLM-proposed relations then optimizes placement and retrieves assets. | Results depend on domain datasets, asset catalogs, and solvers. Plausible furnished rooms are a narrower problem than a playable, art-directed, streamed, performant Unreal level. |
| [SceneFactor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01801), [Controllable Outdoor Scenes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07152) | Research explicitly separates sparse/editable semantic control from dense latent visual synthesis. | Training paired scene-graph/3D-scene data is substantial; generated visuals cannot be treated as authoritative editor state. |
| [SpatialGrammar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27555) | A constrained layout language plus deterministic compiler feedback reduces spatial errors in indoor benchmarks. | Benchmark collision improvements should not be extrapolated to hospitals, terrain, navigation, World Partition, or production acceptance without Hayba-specific tests. |
| [EditBench3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14899), [production-ready 3D survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23629) | Semantic match alone is weak evidence of editability; evaluate locality, multi-view consistency, preservation, and engine-level asset quality separately. | A beautiful point-cloud preview may still destroy non-target edits or fail gameplay and asset hygiene. |

The memory argument is practical as well as conceptual: **illustratively**, 1 million points × a 512-float embedding × 4 bytes is about 2 GB before indices and rendering overhead. A thousand object-level embeddings of the same size are about 2 MB. Real systems may compress features, but the scaling pressure remains; [ConceptGraphs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.16650) motivates object-centric structure on exactly these grounds. Keep detailed point samples local to the selected object/region and use a compact graph for reasoning.

## Proposed product shape

**One source of truth:** a versioned graph, populated from the Unreal world and/or a draft scene plan. Nodes: `world`, `region`, `room/zone`, `route/portal`, `object-slot`, `actor`, `asset`, and optionally `story-beat`. Each node carries a stable ID, transform/bounds or spline/volume proxy, semantic role, state (`observed`, `proposed`, `accepted`, `realized`), evidence source and confidence, owner, and target data layer/cell. Edges: `contains`, `supports`, `adjacent`, `connected_by`, `faces`, `visible_from`, `requires`, `blocks`, `before/after`, with typed parameters and status. The Unreal actor/asset ID is linked rather than duplicated as a fake object. Learned embeddings are auxiliary indices for retrieval, not the authoritative relation store.

**Hospital example:** `ward` contains `bed-slots`; every bed requires bedside clearance and a reachable corridor route; nurse station requires sightline/access to ward; emergency entrance connects to triage. These are explicit room-archetype requirements/relations, not something inferred from a cloud's color. An AI proposes them and cites the brief/template; a designer edits and locks them. Mood is stored at region level as an authored target with reference images and inspectable cues (e.g., cold diffuse light, desaturated palette, sparse props). Aesthetic approval remains a human verdict.

**One UI:** the World view shows a top-down/3D map of proxy shapes, with optional sampled points colored by semantic role or status, edge overlays selected by type, a time/variant selector, and an inspector showing each node's evidence and PLUMB findings. Point samples make volumes, density, and silhouette legible; selecting a cluster resolves to a stable node, not an opaque latent coordinate. The Agent chat can propose a graph diff; the designer accepts a subset, then generates temporary graybox actors or PCG inputs. Explore keeps proposals ephemeral, Draft creates reversible blockouts, and Production promotes only after checks and human review. In Unreal, [PCG points already carry transforms, bounds, color, density, seed, and user attributes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/procedural-content-generation-overview), and [PCG with World Partition can propagate data/HLOD layers](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-pcg-with-world-partition-in-unreal-engine). That makes PCG a practical realization adapter, not the graph database.

**One validation loop:** PLUMB should attach constraints to graph nodes/edges and report a directional margin and fix proposal. Geometry/route checks must use engine facts; inferred labels and mood targets are soft findings until a designer confirms them. A changed node invalidates only dependent checks. The agent proposes changes; the editor records an undoable diff with the exact actor/package effects.

## Hayba-specific blockers seen in the repository

1. Current `HaybaMCPCogMapBuilder.cpp` scans `TActorIterator<AActor>` and classifies cells from class-name substring rules. It has a `TODO` for World Partition. [Epic says partitioned worlds start largely unloaded in the editor](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/world-partition-in-unreal-engine); this map can omit most of a large world. Build a two-tier index: actor descriptors for unloaded coverage, rich component/profile details for loaded actors, and visible `unknown` for unavailable fields. Never show unloaded cells as empty.
2. `src/plumb/primitives.ts` currently describes `clearance` as center-to-center distance, a coarse stand-in until oriented-box overlap exists. `grounded` uses a fixed `z=0` ground plane. Neither is a production physical verdict on sloped landscapes or complex meshes. A production gate needs bounds/overlap and landscape/physics traces, plus navmesh/egress checks relevant to the scene's purpose. Keep current approximations clearly labeled as draft checks.
3. The existing design research note (`docs/design/2026-08-23-extension-redesign/12-AI-3D-RESEARCH.md`) makes strong claims from indoor benchmarks and contains conflicting statements about Unreal-native prior work. Treat it as leads, not proof. For this feature, run controlled Hayba evaluations with real project assets and levels.
4. Stable identity and reconciliation are hard: reimported assets, deleted actors, shifted origin, unloaded cells, duplicated level instances, and designer edits can make a generated graph stale. Version the graph against world snapshot/package IDs; show conflicts instead of silently re-spawning content. [World Partition uses One File Per Actor and data layers](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/world-partition-in-unreal-engine), so promotion must check external actor package persistence, ownership, and streaming setup.

## Narrow MVP and stop conditions

Ship **one room or compact point of interest**, not a whole world: choose a brief, retrieve approved project assets, propose room/zone volumes plus object slots and a few typed relations, display point-sampled proxies, run geometry constraints, let a designer drag/edit/lock nodes, then commit selected graybox actors via an undoable transaction. Start with three templates (hospital ward, outpost, small plaza) and a curated asset catalog. No model training is required for this first version. Use the existing Scene Map, asset index, PLUMB rules, and editor bridge as inputs; add a canonical graph and adapter rather than another panel or `plumb_*` tool family.

Gate a production claim on a preregistered trial with at least 30 fresh briefs across those templates and real project assets, reviewed blind by at least three level designers. Proposed thresholds, to adjust before the trial: **≥80%** of drafts judged worth continuing from; **≥95%** required functional roles/typed relations present; **zero** hard collision, containment, and persistence violations in accepted drafts; **≥95%** preservation of unrelated actors/IDs after a local edit and recompile; reproducible graph-to-graybox output from the same graph/version/seed; and **≥30%** median blockout time saved against a manual baseline. Separately test a partitioned level with unloaded cells and verify that the map distinguishes `unloaded/unknown` from `empty`. These are decision thresholds, not claims that the system currently meets them.

If the first trial misses functional quality but passes deterministic geometry, narrow to a **scene planning and audit** tool rather than automatic layout. If users cannot edit and preserve intent, stop the latent/point-cloud visual investment and ship graph+proxy blockouts only. If the World Partition and PLUMB checks are not upgraded, label output Draft regardless of how persuasive the preview looks.

## Hard no-promises

- No “the AI understands what a hospital is” from co-occurrence alone. Use authored functional templates and explicit designer sign-off.
- No “mood is encoded in dimension five.” Show mood cues and references, ask artists to judge the composition.
- No “latent scene becomes production geometry.” A visual embedding/splat has no inherent collision, rigging, UV/PBR, streaming, provenance, or performance budget. [The production-readiness survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23629) documents these gaps.
- No “all actors are represented” until unloaded World Partition cells and level instances are indexed and tested.
- No “zero collisions” from center-distance checks or one indoor research benchmark.

**Recommendation:** call the feature *Scene Plan* or *Scene Graph* in the UI. Its differentiator is a reversible, inspectable path from intent → blockout → verified Unreal changes. “Latent space” can describe the optional semantic retrieval backend, not the promise to a level designer.
